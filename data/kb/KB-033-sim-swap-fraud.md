## Symptoms
- SIM suddenly stopped working and the customer did not request a replacement.
- Customer received SMS about a SIM change, eSIM, or porting they did not ask for.
- Customer reports OTPs received for transactions they did not make, or money lost from the bank account.

## Likely causes
- Fraudster obtained a duplicate SIM with forged documents or social engineering.
- Phishing - the customer shared an OTP or the UPC code.

## Steps
1. Treat as urgent. Verify the caller with the security questions before discussing the account.
2. Immediately **block** the SIM/eSIM and suspend outgoing services on the number.
3. Stop any pending porting request.
4. Advise the customer to inform their bank and freeze UPI/net banking right away, and to report the fraud on the national cyber crime helpline (1930).
5. Mark the case as **critical - fraud/security**. Restoration with a new SIM happens only after in-store verification with original ID, and the resolution needs admin approval.
6. Never share details of who requested the SIM change or which store issued it; the fraud team handles that.

## Escalate when
- Always. Fraud cases go to the fraud team and need admin approval.
